Test-time Anatomical Canonicalization

A structure detector trained at one hospital, adapted to another on a single unlabeled image, with no training and no access to source data. Four steps, animated.

STEP 1 · THE PROBLEM

The anatomy arrives at the wrong size, so it lands on the wrong pyramid level

A detector resolves an object on one level of its feature pyramid, chosen from the object's area by a fixed rule. Train at one center and objects fall on the levels the detector learned. Change vendor, depth or zoom and the same anatomy, unchanged, moves to levels it was never trained for. The bars below are measured on the four-chamber view, one center's model applied to another.

STEP 2 · ANI

Every box votes for the one factor that would return it to its canonical size

The detector's own first pass is the only evidence. Each box reports a size and claims a structure; the source prior says how large that structure usually is. The gap between the two is a vote. Votes are weighted by how plausible the claimed size is, so boxes that landed on nothing lose their say instead of having to be thresholded away. What plays below is the deployed estimator on a real image, with its own intermediate values.

STEP 3 · UVA

How sharp the estimate is decides how far apart the extra views go

The same objective that gives the factor gives its curvature, and the curvature gives the width of a Gaussian surrogate. Views are placed at the quadrature nodes of that surrogate, so a confident image is bracketed tightly and an ambiguous one wide. The width is not a hyper-parameter; only the two clip bounds are.

STEP 4 · SDF

Fuse the views, then let the layout of the plane settle the duplicates

Boxes of one structure from several views are averaged with their confidences as weights, and the fused confidence is divided by the number of views rather than by the size of the cluster, so a box seen once is discounted against one confirmed by several. One box per structure is then chosen under a prior on where structures sit relative to each other. The loser is down-weighted, never deleted.
